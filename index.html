Olena Sobolieva

Social Anthropologist

Political Violence · Knowledge · Cultural Heritage · Crimea

Exploring how political violence transforms knowledge, cultural heritage, and social relationships, through long-term ethnographic engagement with Crimea and the Black Sea region.

University of Basel · Lecturer in Social Anthropology

Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin · INHERIT Fellow, 2026–2027

Olena Sobolieva is a social anthropologist with twenty years of research in Ukraine and Eastern Europe. Since her first fieldwork in Crimea in 2003, her work has centred on the Crimean Tatars: deportation and return, memory and land, sacred places and lived Islam, wedding ritual and foodways. Her ethnographic corpus, gathered before 2014, includes more than 300 interviews with elders and the documentation of some 50 local sacred sites (aziz).

She is the author of two books on Crimean Tatar culture, co-founded and directs the Centre for Applied Anthropology in Kyiv, and co-edited a special section on Ukraine in Anthropology and Humanism (2026).

Olena Sobolieva

Social Anthropologist

Political Violence · Knowledge · Cultural Heritage · Crimea

Exploring how political violence transforms knowledge, cultural heritage, and social relationships, through long-term ethnographic engagement with Crimea and the Black Sea region.

University of Basel · Lecturer in Social Anthropology

Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin · INHERIT Fellow, 2026–2027

Olena Sobolieva is a social anthropologist and ethnologist whose research explores the relationships between political violence, knowledge, cultural heritage, and displacement. Drawing on more than two decades of ethnographic engagement with Crimea and Crimean Tatar communities, she examines how occupation and forced displacement transform the conditions of knowing, remembering, and maintaining social relationships.

She is currently an INHERIT Fellow at Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin and a Lecturer at the University of Basel. Previously, she held a research fellowship at the University of Basel and worked at research institutions in Ukraine, including the National Research Institute of Ukrainian Studies and the Institute of Art Studies, Folklore and Ethnology of the National Academy of Sciences of Ukraine.

Her research combines long-term ethnographic fieldwork, archival materials, and methodological reflection on the possibilities and limits of anthropology under conditions of political violence. She is the author of two books on Crimean Tatar cultural practices and heritage, and co-founder of the Centre for Applied Anthropology in Kyiv.
